For concentrated-liquidity AMMs such as Uniswap V3, the defining variable of an LP position is often not the APR.

It is the price range.

Suppose ETH is trading at 3,000 USDC.

Providing ETH/USDC liquidity between:

`2,800–3,200`

and

`2,000–5,000`

creates two fundamentally different positions.

A narrower range concentrates more capital around the current price and therefore increases capital efficiency.

If ETH keeps trading back and forth between 2,800 and 3,200, your liquidity participates in more swaps and can potentially earn more fees.

But the trade-off is equally clear.

- As ETH rises, your ETH is gradually sold into USDC.
- As ETH falls, your USDC is gradually converted into ETH.
- Above 3,200, the position eventually becomes almost entirely USDC.
- Below 2,800, it becomes almost entirely ETH.

And once price moves outside your range, the position stops earning swap fees until price comes back into range or you reposition the liquidity.

That is why I now think the first question about an LP position should not be:

> “What APR does this range offer?”

Instead, I ask:

- If price reaches the upper bound, am I comfortable effectively selling the asset?
- If price reaches the lower bound, am I comfortable converting my stablecoins into that asset?

If the answer to both is yes, the range may actually make sense.

## A Narrower Range Is Not Automatically Better

A narrow range simply concentrates more liquidity into a smaller price interval.

That improves capital efficiency, but it also makes the position more sensitive to price movement.

When price stays inside the range and keeps oscillating, LPs can do very well.

But once a strong directional move develops, the position converts into a single asset much faster and can quickly move out of range.

So the goal is not to perfectly predict the exact top and bottom.

The goal is to find a range where:

**price has a reasonable probability of spending time, and you are comfortable with the asset conversion that happens at both boundaries.**

## What About Meme Coins?

The immediate reaction is often:

> “Meme coins are too volatile. Impermanent loss is too high. LPing them makes no sense.”

I think that conclusion is too simplistic.

Meme coins are volatile, but they also tend to have extremely high turnover.

And high turnover can generate substantial trading fees.

So Meme LPs can absolutely have value.

The important distinction is that:

**the source of your return and the source of your risk are different.**

- **You earn:** fees generated by trading volume.
- **You risk:** inventory being transformed by persistent directional price movement.

Imagine a Meme coin trades like this:

`1 → 0.8 → 1.1 → 0.9 → 1.05`

There is a lot of volatility and turnover, but the final price has not moved very far.

That type of environment can be relatively attractive for an LP.

Traders keep crossing the pool, while the LP keeps collecting fees.

Now compare that with:

`1 → 1.5 → 3 → 5 → 10`

During the rally, your Meme inventory is continuously sold into stablecoins.

The token may eventually be up 10x, while your LP position has already converted most of the exposure into stablecoins.

The opposite is also true.

If price goes:

`1 → 0.5 → 0.2 → 0.05`

the LP continuously uses stablecoins to buy the falling token.

Eventually, you can end up holding a large directional position in the Meme coin.

That is why the biggest problem for a Meme LP is not simply “high volatility.”

It is:

**high volatility combined with a strong directional trend.**

High-turnover sideways markets can be attractive for LPs.

Persistent rallies or persistent declines can turn LPs into the counterparty of trend-following traders.

And even sideways volatility is not guaranteed profit.

Fees still need to compensate for impermanent loss / LVR, gas costs, rebalancing costs and the tail risk of the token itself.

## So My LP Evaluation Order Has Changed

I now look at:

**Price structure → Range → Trading volume → APR**

not:

APR → Deposit capital.

A pool may show 100% APR, but if the token is in a strong one-way rally, I may still avoid it.

Meanwhile, a 30% APR pool with stable volume, persistent range-bound trading and a manageable liquidity range may actually be a much better market-making opportunity.

This is especially true for Meme coins.

I prefer to think of Meme LPs as:

**short-duration market-making strategies**

rather than:

“depositing tokens and waiting for yield.”

A simple mental model for LPing is:

> I am willing to continuously sell into strength and buy into weakness inside this range. The AMM executes the inventory conversion automatically, and I receive trading fees in return.

Once you think about LPs this way, the trade-off becomes much clearer.

**Think a Meme coin can go 10x?**
Holding the token may be the cleaner trade.

**Think it will churn inside a range with heavy turnover?**
That is where LPing starts to become interesting.

LP yield is never free.

Trading fees are compensation for taking inventory risk and providing liquidity to the market.
